# Financial Statement & Ratio Analysis

## Aravind Consumer Products Ltd.

A comprehensive financial statement and ratio analysis project developed to evaluate the financial health, profitability, liquidity, leverage, efficiency and cash-generation capability of a fictional Indian consumer-products company.

---

## 📊 Project Overview

This project analyzes the financial performance of **Aravind Consumer Products Ltd.**, a fictional Indian consumer-products company with operations across multiple business segments.

The analysis converts financial statements into meaningful business and management insights using:

- Income Statement Analysis
- Balance Sheet Analysis
- Cash Flow Analysis
- Profitability Ratios
- Liquidity Ratios
- Leverage Ratios
- Efficiency Ratios
- Working Capital Analysis
- DuPont Analysis
- Segment Analysis
- Peer Benchmarking
- Financial Risk Analysis
- KPI Development
- Strategic Financial Recommendations

The project covers a modeled period from **FY2022 to FY2026E**.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

The primary objectives are to:

1. Evaluate revenue and profit growth.

3. Assess short-term liquidity.

# 📈 Profitability Analysis

The project evaluates:

### Gross Margin

**Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue × 100**

Measures the profitability of the company's core products before operating expenses.

### EBIT Margin

**EBIT Margin = EBIT / Revenue × 100**

Measures operating profitability.

### Net Profit Margin

**Net Margin = Net Profit / Revenue × 100**

Measures the percentage of revenue converted into net profit.

### ROA

**ROA = Net Income / Total Assets**

Measures how efficiently the company generates profit from its asset base.

### ROE

**ROE = Net Income / Shareholders' Equity**

Measures the return generated on shareholder capital.

---

# 💧 Liquidity Analysis

Liquidity is evaluated using:

## Current Ratio

**Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities**

The modeled company maintains a current ratio above 2x, indicating a relatively strong short-term liquidity position.

## Quick Ratio

The quick ratio evaluates the company's ability to meet short-term obligations without relying heavily on inventory liquidation.

## Working Capital

**Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities**

Positive working capital provides an operating liquidity cushion, although excessive working capital can also indicate inefficient use of capital.

---

# 🏦 Leverage Analysis

The project evaluates:

- Debt-to-Equity
- Debt-to-EBITDA
- Interest Coverage

## Debt-to-Equity

**Debt-to-Equity = Total Debt / Equity**

Measures the extent to which the business relies on debt financing.

## Interest Coverage

**Interest Coverage = EBIT / Interest Expense**

Measures the company's ability to service interest obligations from operating earnings.

FY2025 represents the weakest modeled leverage position, while FY2026E assumes gradual deleveraging.

---

# ⚙️ Efficiency Analysis

The project evaluates:

### Asset Turnover

Measures revenue generated from the company's asset base.

### Inventory Turnover

Measures how efficiently inventory is converted into sales.

### Receivables Turnover

Measures the efficiency of collecting customer receivables.

Efficiency improvements can release working capital and improve return on invested capital.

- Operating Cash Flow

## Free Cash Flow

**Free Cash Flow = Operating Cash Flow − Capital Expenditure**

FY2025 shows negative free cash flow because of elevated capital expenditure.

FY2026E models stronger operating cash generation and positive free cash flow.

# 📐 DuPont Analysis

DuPont analysis decomposes ROE into three components:

**ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier**

This framework helps identify whether shareholder returns are driven by:

- Profitability
- Asset efficiency
- Financial leverage

The analysis indicates that improving operating profitability is more important than relying excessively on additional leverage.

# ⚠️ Financial Risk Analysis

Key risks identified include:

### 1. Margin Compression
Rising costs or pricing pressure could reduce profitability.

### 2. Leverage Risk
Higher debt can increase financial risk and interest obligations.

### 3. Working Capital Risk
Excess inventory or slower receivable collection can consume cash.

### 4. Capex Risk
Large capital investments may reduce short-term free cash flow.

### 5. Demand Risk
Slower consumer demand could affect revenue growth.

### 6. Cash Flow Risk
Profit growth without corresponding cash generation can create financial pressure.

# 💡 Strategic Financial Recommendations

Based on the analysis:

1. Protect gross margins through pricing and product-mix management.
2. Improve inventory turnover.
3. Strengthen receivables collection.
4. Maintain disciplined capital expenditure.
5. Gradually reduce leverage using free cash flow.
6. Monitor interest coverage closely.
7. Improve profitability of weaker business segments.
8. Prioritize investments with attractive returns.
9. Improve free-cash-flow conversion.
